Adams, Terri M. and Douglas B Fuller. 2006. “The Words Have Changed but the Ideology Remains the Same: Misogynistic Lyrics in Rap Music.” Journal of Black Studies 36 (6) https://doi.org/10.1177/0021934704274072.

The source, published in the Journal of Black Studies by two scholars from Howard University, examines misogynistic themes in rap music, such as some of those expressed by A$AP Ferg in his song “Plain Jane”. The piece connects the role misogyny plays in rap over time, as well as connects the roles both men and women play in the genre in contributing towards this perpetuating theme, as well as the impact this has on communities and the genre. This analysis is done by examining lyrics of Billboard’s top 15 rap songs between 1989 and 2001. I hope to use this source to examine the collaboration between A$AP Ferg and Nikki Minaj on “Plain Jane”, and examine the visuals as well as lyrics that may highlight some of the sentiments expressed in this piece. 

Ferguson, Darold. 2017. “A$AP Ferg "Plain Jane" Official Lyrics & Meaning” Verified. Genius. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eISsaQNGnAU&ab_channel=Genius.

This video is an interview of A$AP Ferg, the artist of the song “Plain Jane” talks about the motivations behind making the song, as well as explains some of the lyrics and hidden messages and meanings within the song. He talks about how he was inspired by the song “Slob on my Knob” by the Three Six Mafia, and the greater hiphop movies at the time. The interview reveals a lot about Ferg’s character and work ethic, which is highlighted as he talks through the lyrics from his song and expands on his personal insights. The discussion is definitely insightful, especially coming from the artist himself. I will use this source to understand Ferg’s inspirations and gain a better understanding of the language he uses and the significance behind it.

Guyton, Mikalah. 2021, “Gangsta Rap and the Trapped Mentality.” Georgia Southern University Commons, March 25. https://digitalcommons.georgiasouthern.edu/honors-theses/572.

The source is by a musicologist at Georgia Southern University, which talks about the history of hip-hop and how gangsta rap and trap music would branch out from this due to historical events such as the crack epidemic of the 1980s. Guyton then investigates five common themes that occur in such genres of music: masculinity, respect, violence and retaliation, misogyny, and wealth, and argues that the genre exploits challenges faced by African American communities, and although it helps unite communities through lived experiences, the themes perpetuate a cycle of problematic messages in today's world. I hope to use this source to understand some of the themes and further understand some of the lyrics used by Ferg in his song “Plain Jane”. This paper can help me investigate some of the underlying themes in Ferg’s music.

Ruiz, Mathhew Ismael. 2017. “Still Striving” Pitchfork, August 21. https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/adollarap-ferg-still-striving/.

This is a review of A$AP Ferg’s album, which contains the song “Plain Jane”. While the critic argues that the album is a bit underwhelming in terms of Ferg’s own verses, as he includes many guest artists, he also says that the labelling of the album as a mixtape may be a purposeful move to show Ferg’s very raw and real nature. I think this song is useful as it highlights the background of “Plain Jane” (it was originally a remastering of an older hip-hop song), but it also gives a lot of insight into how others perceive Ferg as an artist and how they align with his artistic vision or not. 

Thompson, Paul. 2021. “Devil and the Details: The Undying Influence of Three 6 Mafia.” The Ringer, December 17. https://www.theringer.com/2021/12/17/music/three-6-mafia-influence-2010s.

This is an article published on The Ringer, a pop culture website by Paul Thompson (senior editor of the Los Angeles Review of Books and published in Rolling Stone and GQ) that examines the impact of the Memphis-based rap group Three 6 Mafia. He argues that the group’s lyrical style and flow laid the groundwork for modern rap artists (such as A$AP Ferg, who used the song “Slob On my Knob” as inspiration for “Plain Jane”) such as A$AP Mob members Ferg and Rocky. The article works to map-out some of the notable elements of Three 6 Mafia’s work, and how its legacies have carried into the modern day. The piece references how A$AP Rocky’s flow is heavily influenced by members of the group, and how Legendary rap group Migos has a similar triplet style to them. This is a very interesting article to explore for my project on “Plain Jane”, as it references the artists that wrote the song “Plain Jane” is based off of. The content in the piece (as described above) can help explain a lot of the stylistic choices used by Ferg in his music and its roots in Three 6 Mafia’s work, and it can explain how the popularity of “Slob on my Knob” fuelled “Plain Jane”’s rise.

 

Bradley, Regina N. 2015. “Barbz and kings: explorations of gender and sexuality in hip-hop.” Cambridge University Press, February 5th. https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-companion-to-hiphop/barbz-and-kings-explorations-of-gender-and-sexuality-in-hiphop/2B014C7439316C5F6BD01297369751A4.

In this article by the Cambridge University Press, Regina Bradley, Associate Professor of English and African Diaspora Studies at Kennesaw State University, (https://facultyweb.kennesaw.edu/rbradl14/untitled.php) explores how gender and sexuality expressed within hip-hop culture and the history behind that. She focuses on fans of female artists like Nicki Minaj and contrasts it with representations of masculine artists in the genre. She references artist interviews and fan practices for the artists and uses these to investigate how artists and their audiences engage differently with different identities. This source is relevant to my project because it can help me understand how hip-hop artists like A$AP Ferg navigate masculinity in their music and visuals, and maybe how Ferg tries to position himself as an influential voice in rap.

Neblett, Touré and Darold Ferguson. 2025. “ASAP Ferg–I Am Darold.” Toure Show. Season 1, Episode 460. DCP Entertainment, January 1. https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/asap-ferg-i-am-darold/id1313077481.

He also discusses his affiliation with the A$AP Mob, a rap group he founded alongside other Harlem-based rappers, and which helped put him and others into the spotlight. He discusses how this is no longer who he is, but cites it as an important part of his come-up. This is particularly interesting because it ties into my playlist, which incorporates songs from A$AP Mob members, and it could be interesting to see where that could take me, in terms of investigating themes of togetherness and friendship in the genre, or, use it to tell a story with my playlist. The episode also highlights some of Ferg’s experiences, which could provide some insight into analyzing “Plain Jane” lyrically, as there are still some lyrics I am uncertain about.

Price Jr., Robert J. 2005. “Hegemony, Hope, and the Harlem Renaissance: Taking Hip Hop Culture Seriously.” Adult Education Research Conference, Athens, GA. https://newprairiepress.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?referer=&httpsredir=1&article=2681&context=aerc.

In this paper, presented at an education conference, author Robert J. Price Jr (an Associate Professor of Evangelism and Urban Ministry at University of Illinois) highlights connections between rap/hip-hop culture and the Harlem Renaissance (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_Renaissance), and that this music is culturally significant in embodying traditions and history of African American culture. He also discusses how the music works to empower people in these communities and as an outlet for them by using his own interpretation for historical texts to frame hip-hop as a response to systemic oppression. Finally, he highlights the importance of context of this music, such as what social events were occurring at the time. The source ties into A$AP Ferg because he is a profound user of references in his songs to events in his life and other world events / celebrities. I think this source would be useful in helping analyze his message and purpose for “Plain Jane” and his other songs.

“A New African American Identity: The Harlem Renaissance.” Smithsonian. Accessed March 13, 2025. https://nmaahc.si.edu/explore/stories/new-african-american-identity-harlem-renaissance.

The article published by the Smithsonian Institute’s National Museum of African American History and Culture explores the Harlem Renaissance, a cultural movement in the 1920-30s in which black art in all forms exploded, which fostered a sense of identity and pride for African Americans (which originated in Harlem, as the name suggests). The website uses images of notable figures to push the story, impact, and legacy of the movement. The article is useful for understanding underlying causes that influenced modern African American music and art, especially because A$AP Ferg has backgrounds in both. Finally, the article provides insight into Harlem’s (where A$AP Ferg grew up) cultural legacy, which continues to influence artists from this community, such as Ferg.

 

Mutinda, Brinta. 2020. “Understanding Hip-Hop Through Live Performances.” Medium, October 22. https://medium.com/@mutindabrinta/understanding-hip-hop-through-live-performances-a77deb7cb143.

The article explores how methods of performance by artists are important in maintaining the authenticity of hip-hop music. Mutinda takes her own perspective and notable events in hip-hop to describe how hip-hop originated from community gatherings in black-dominant neighborhoods, and this is the reason why the performance aspect is so significant in this genre. The article is published on Medium, which is a blog-style journalism platform about pop-culture and the author is a History and International Relations student at the London School of Economics, which gives the source some credibility. This source also helps put into context the Music Video for “Plain Jane”, why it depicts Ferg in Harlem making live performances, interacting with the community he came up from.

2021. “Sampling: Its Role In Hip Hop & Its Legacy In Music Production.” Abbey Road Institute, February 4. https://abbeyroadinstitute.co.uk/blog/sampling-role-in-hip-hop-and-its-legacy-in-music-production/.

The Abbey Road Institute is a music education institute that teaches about production, and the article is published on their website. The article discusses the history and role of sampling in hip-hop music and how it helped the genre grow. From the early DJs in neighborhoods to producers now whose beats get millions of listens. By referencing notable artists over the years and frequently asked questions about sampling, it talks about how sampling passes on traditions in hip-hop and that it's used as a sort of storytelling and can help you trace mentorship, influence, etc. This source will help my project as Ferg is a profound sampler, not just for “Plain Jane” but many other songs such as “Work” in the album “Trap Lord”, it also pushes my argument that Ferg’s use of this alongside his remixing work allows for innovation and inclusion in the genre while preserving its roots.
