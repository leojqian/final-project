"Slob on My Knob" to "Plain Jane" - A$AP Ferg's Artistic Reinterpretation and Evolution - Final Essay

In 2017, rapper and trapstar A$AP Ferg released "Plain Jane" as part of "Still Striving," his latest mixtape. The title track of the mixtape remastered Juicy J of Three 6 Mafia's rap hit "Slob On My Knob" from 1999, becoming an instant hit, igniting party scenes, sparking dance challenges, and earning platinum certification from Billboard. Beneath “Plain Jane’s” infectious beat, there lies an artistic vision that is rooted in Ferg’s childhood, influences from artists across the country, and a display of hip-hop’s evolution across generations, which work together to reveal that rap thrives on creative reinterpretations of its past that upholds its core traditions and can move the genre to become more inclusive.

A$AP Ferg was born Darold Ferguson Jr. in Harlem, New York. Before he began rapping, Ferg attended art school and first rose to prominence in fashion design when he founded Devoni Clothing, an apparel line that specialized in jewelry and belts (Ismael 2017). In his interviews, Ferg spoke about growing up around other creative figures like his father, Darold Ferguson, who owned a Harlem boutique from which he designed logos and t-shirts for record labels such as Bad Boy Records. (Touré 2025). His exposure to art throughout his life through fashion and art undoubtedly created the foundation for Ferg's music later on, where the aesthetics of his music videos and musical production would become staple elements of his artistic vision. Ferg immerses his audience not just with audio but also with visuals and thematics. His cross-disciplinary approach through visual and aural immersion enables the success of "Plain Jane" through its popularized lyrics and modern sounds, alongside his other songs.

Ferg's unique artistic vision is clearly seen in “Plain Jane”, taking inspiration from “Slob on my Knob,” a song by Memphis rap group Three 6 Mafia (Ferguson 2017). The rap group was credited with being "20 years ahead of its time on equipment that was perhaps 10 years behind," due to the group rising out of poverty (Thompson 2021). Their songs are characterized by rolling bass lines and the innovative triplet flow beats, pioneered by member Lord Infamous, which established a precedent that would carry on with future generations of artists in the genre. Such innovations caused the Three 6 Mafia's influence to extend far beyond Memphis and the south. They popularized the use of mixtapes to distribute their music through “underground” means, which Ferg would utilize to release “Still Striving,” showing the influence that Three 6 Mafia would continue to have long after their disbandment. His use of both their lyrics and style works to preserve the heritage of the genre while providing innovation through modernization.

Rather than creating a remix, Ferg preserves recognizable phrases from Juicy J's iconic verses while updating the production with contemporary trap elements for his production of "Plain Jane". The original's sparse, synth-like production gives way to Kirk Knight's (credited as Kirk Kneezy in the song) bass-heavy beat. In my eyes, Ferg is creating bridges that span regions and eras. His adaptation harnesses the energy of the original song while making it more accessible to the current audience. Ferg embodies the vision of Three 6 Mafia, how they "pushed the genre into more acrobatic modes and grimier spaces, urging young artists to build the future with raw materials from the past" (Thompson 2021). Without such a process of transformation, regional innovations would not be able to become mainstream as “Plain Jane” did. Ferg demonstrates that these transformations enrich and preserve, rather than dilute, the hip-hop genre. 

By reworking the opening line "Slob on my knob" into the now-iconic "Ride with the mob," Ferg simultaneously pays homage to and transforms the original lyric by maintaining the rhyme and references the theme of togetherness in “Slob on my Knob”. Such transformations echo the artistic innovation and exploration that exploded during the Harlem Renaissance (Smithsonian, 2025), when, as Robert Price notes, artists adapted existing cultural elements to "counter hegemonic forces" and inspire "hope and vision" (Price 2005, 3). These transitions blend hip-hop into a continuous genre with classic themes but modern beats. Each new generation of artists maintains connections to those who came before in terms of sampling, mentorship, or brotherhood, and continues to innovate in the genre. Paying homage to his mentor A$AP Yams and Ferg's inspiration from Three 6 Mafia, Ferg demonstrates how sampling and reinterpretation serve as cultural memory, maintaining the essence and authenticity of the genre. 

Alongside the mixtape release, Ferg also released a remix of “Plain Jane” that featured Nicki Minaj, which added another dimension to the song's cultural significance. Scholars have argued that rap frequently contains lyrics and themes that objectify women (Adams and Fuller 2006, 948). Ferg and Minaj's collaboration disrupts this pattern, as explored in "Breaking Boundaries and Honoring Roots (Music Review)", with her verses asserting feminine power and positivity in a genre with a culture that is  dominated by masculine perspectives. Such a collaboration between notable artists represents how adaptations of music over time can not only preserve but also transform traditions that have changed connotations. Through the remix, Minaj and Ferg create a more inclusive dialogue for the audience that maintains the connection to hip-hop's past.

Overall, "Plain Jane" illustrates that hip-hop evolves through creative reinterpretation of its past. A$AP Ferg's artistic upbringing allows him to create a piece that honors its southern roots and hip-hop’s essence while speaking to contemporary audiences. His remastering of “Slob on my Knob” to “Plain Jane” embodies how hip-hop stays simultaneously rooted in tradition and innovation, creating a continuous conversation between its past and present. In today’s diversified cultural landscape, A$AP Ferg’s demonstration of how we can transition from past to present offers a blueprint not just for hip-hop, but also for how we might adapt to cultural changes to our society in the future.
