Introduction - The Cultural Significance of A$AP Ferg's "Plain Jane"

In 2017, Harlem-based rapper A$AP Ferg released his song "Plain Jane” under the album Still Striving, a track that would quickly become one of his most recognizable and defining works. On the surface, this bass-heavy trap anthem appears to be yet another example of the rap genre’s glorification of wealth and masculinity. Through analysis of the song, "Plain Jane" serves to demonstrate how artists can push genres forwards with modern production techniques while simultaneously honoring hip-hop's roots, by gradually and symbolically breaking down barriers that have characterized the rap genre since its inception. What truly makes this song unique is that it pushes for change while maintaining trap music’s essence. Ferg connects the past and present of hip-hop while simultaneously reflecting and challenging themes in rap demonstrates the potential for evolution in a genre that is often criticized for stagnant sounds and beliefs. 

As explored in the Critical Reading Assignment of Mikalah Guyton's "Gangsta Rap and the Trapped Mentality," trap music emerged from socioeconomic conditions in primarily African American communities, acting as a way for aspiring artists to work their way out of poverty through a process of “struggle, grind, and shine”. Guyton identifies six recurring themes in trap music: "masculinity, respect, violence and retaliation, misogyny, wealth, and drugs." (Guyton 2021). By analyzing "Plain Jane", I was also able to identify such themes in A$AP Ferg’s music. However, Ferg’s collaboration verse of “Plain Jane” with rap icon Nicki Minaj, reveals how he navigates these such themes with awareness and a progressive mindset, aiming to break down misogynistic barriers in the hip-hop scene. As a result of this collaboration, it fuelled social media trends in light of the #MeToo movement and worked to empower women globally. This is a distinguishing characteristic that separates his work from his contemporaries. As Guyton argues, many trap artists remain "mentally trapped" in the personas they've constructed (and that the genre has perpetuated), perpetuating problematic narratives even after achieving success. Ferg's approach in "Plain Jane" demonstrates a more nuanced relationship with his roots and with the genre as a whole. He is able to maintain the masculine identity that has become synonymous with the genre while also pushing a progressive message towards his female audience.

As explored in the Music Review assignment, “Plain Jane’s” music video reinforces Ferg's authentic connection to his Harlem origins. By filming in the streets where he grew up and came up, as well as featuring real clips from this community, Ferg creates a "window into a new reality" (Adams and Fuller 2006) for listeners outside his community. The immersive visuals are able to include the audience in Ferg’s mob as he rides (ride with the mob, Alhamdulillah). However, unlike the purely exploitative approach that Adams and Fuller critique in their analysis of misogynistic rap lyrics (Adams and Fuller 2006), Ferg's music video is not for the glorification of his past, instead he uses the work to highlight issues that plagues the community that he came from by acknowledges the struggles his community faces as a form of respect to the people that brought him up. Overall, the visuals of the music video are able to create a very real image of this community.

Finally, the Listening Guide contextualizes “Plain Jane” for the reader by placing them in the shoes of an up-and-coming rapper, using other hit rap songs by A$AP Ferg and other artists to highlight the trials and tribulations on their journeys to success. The playlist explores themes of faith, perseverance, pain, and finally, success, in an attempt to ground the song in lived experiences rather than taking everything as face-value. As the reader will come to realize, there is much more behind the manufactured bravado of Ferg: he throws in many hidden meanings behind his lyrics and there is always a hidden inspiration and homage behind each track. 

Ultimately, "Plain Jane’s" significance lies precisely in its liminality. Through this song any my analysis of it, we’ve uncovered that Ferg is able to straddle both hip-hop's past and future while both reflecting and challenging perpetuated themes in rap, and demonstrating the potential for evolution in a genre that is often criticized for stagnant sounds and beliefs. By situating "Plain Jane" within these overlapping contexts—its relationship to trap music traditions, its homage to hip-hop's past, and its potential for challenging problematic norms—-we gain a more nuanced understanding of not just this individual song, but of hip-hop's ongoing evolution as both an art form and cultural influence.

An aspect of "Plain Jane" that cannot be ignored is the song's ties to hip-hop's past. By sampling and referencing Three 6 Mafia's "Slob on My Knob," Ferg is able to produce an innovative song through revitalizing older hit tracks and taking inspiration from other great artists. (Ismael 2017) Ferg recycles some Three 6 Mafia’s original lyrics but includes his own verses (Ferguson 2017). This practice connects directly to hip-hop's origins as described by Guyton, where early DJs in hip-hop would "extend break beats from soul records to virtually generate a new song." (Guyton 2021) Ferg's modernization of Three 6 Mafia's flow represents a continuation of this foundational hip-hop practice.
