Introduction - The Cultural Significance of A$AP Ferg's "Plain Jane"

In 2017, Harlem-based rapper A$AP Ferg released his song "Plain Jane” in the album Still Striving. The song would quickly become one of his most recognizable and defining works. On the surface, this bass-heavy trap anthem appears to be yet another example of the rap genre’s glorification of wealth and masculinity. "Plain Jane" serves to demonstrate how artists can push genres forwards with modern production techniques while simultaneously honoring hip-hop's roots, by gradually and symbolically breaking down barriers that have characterized the genre since its inception. This song is truly unique because it pushes for change while maintaining trap music’s essence. Ferg connects the past and present of hip-hop while both mirroring and challenging themes in rap, which demonstrates potential for progress in a genre that is often criticized for its stagnant sounds and beliefs. 

Trap music emerged from socioeconomic conditions in primarily African American communities in the 1970s, acting as a way for aspiring artists to work their way out of poverty through a process of “struggle, grind, and shine.” As explored in the Critical Reading Assignment, author Mikalah Guyton, a scholar at Georgia Southern University, identifies six recurring themes in trap music: "masculinity, respect, violence and retaliation, misogyny, wealth, and drugs" (Guyton 2021). Plain Jane' shows how these themes are also present in A$AP Ferg's music. However, Ferg’s collaboration verse of “Plain Jane” with rap icon Nicki Minaj reveals how he navigates such themes with awareness and a progressive mindset, aiming to break down misogynistic barriers in the hip-hop scene. This collaboration fuelled social media trends in light of the #MeToo movement and worked to empower women globally. This is a distinguishing characteristic that separates Ferg’s work from that of his contemporaries. As Guyton argues, many trap artists remain "mentally trapped" in the personas they've constructed, perpetuating problematic narratives even after achieving success. Ferg's approach in "Plain Jane" demonstrates a more nuanced relationship with his roots and with the genre as a whole. He is able to maintain the masculine identity that has become synonymous with the genre while also pushing a progressive message towards his female audience.

“Plain Jane’s” music video reinforces Ferg's authentic connection to his Harlem origins, which honors the importance of community and tradition in rap. By filming in the streets where he came up, as well as featuring real clips from this community, Ferg creates an insight into where he grew up for listeners outside his community, minus the stereotypes. The immersive visuals are able to include the audience in Ferg’s mob as he rides (ride with the mob, Alhamdulillah). However, unlike the purely exploitative approach that Adams and Fuller critique in their analysis of misogynistic rap lyrics (Adams and Fuller 2006), Ferg's music video is not for the glorification of his past; instead, he uses the work to highlight issues that plagues the community that he came from by acknowledges the struggles his community faces as a form of respect to the people that brought him up. By grounding a southern-inspired song in the authenticity of Harlem, Ferg demonstrates how creative transformation translates the original context into his own authentic experience. When Ferg combines southern flow patterns with New York imagery, he creates cross-regional dialogue that sparked hip-hop's diverse traditions.

The Listening Guide contextualizes “Plain Jane” for the reader by placing them in the shoes of an up-and-coming rapper, using other hit rap songs by A$AP Ferg and other artists to highlight the trials and tribulations on their journeys to success. The playlist explores themes of faith, perseverance, pain, and, finally, success in an attempt to ground the song in lived experiences rather than taking everything at face-value. As the reader will come to realize, there is much more behind the manufactured bravado of Ferg: he throws in many hidden meanings behind his lyrics and there is always a hidden inspiration and homage behind each track. 

Diving deeper into Ferg’s hit song the Final Essay examines how "Plain Jane" exemplifies the evolution of hip-hop through creative reinterpretation rather than simple imitation. A$AP Ferg's artistic upbringing in a creative household, where his father designed logos and clothing for record labels like Bad Boy Records, provided him with a multidisciplinary approach to music that extends beyond just remixing sounds to include visual aesthetics and audience inclusion in his videos and lyrics. His modernization of Three 6 Mafia's "Slob On My Knob" into "Plain Jane" demonstrates how hip-hop preserves its heritage while embracing innovation, creating is described as "bridges that span regions and eras" (Thompson 2021). Such a delicate balance between tradition and innovation illustrates how hip-hop remains culturally relevant by continuously engaging in dialogue with its own history while adapting to changing social contexts of the modern day. 

Ultimately, "Plain Jane’s" significance lies precisely in its liminality. Ferg is able to straddle both hip-hop's past and future while both reflecting and challenging perpetuated themes in rap, and demonstrating the potential for evolution in a genre that is often criticized for stagnant sounds and beliefs. By situating "Plain Jane" within these overlapping contexts—its relationship to trap music traditions, its homage to hip-hop's past, and its potential for challenging problematic norms—-one gains a more nuanced understanding of not just this individual song, but of hip-hop's ongoing evolution as both an art form and cultural influence.
