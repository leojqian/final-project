Critical Reading Assignment

In A$AP Ferg’s rap song, “Plain Jane”, Ferg raps heavily about his personal success and upbringing in an adaptation of the classic rap song “Slob on my Knob” by Three 6 Mafia. In the piece “Gangsta Rap and the Trapped Mentality”, Mikalah Guyton examines how gangsta rap and trap music, both genres of which A$AP Ferg is a prominent artist, shifted over time, and how common themes in the genre, such as “(1) masculinity, (2) respect, (3) violence and retaliation, (4) misogyny, (5) wealth, and (6) drugs” (Guyton 2021, 1) emerged. Guyton argues that in both genres, music acts as an outlet and route to success for those that feel trapped and come from typically African American backgrounds, however, the messages the music convey may be problematic, as they promote negative behaviors. The artists rap about how they overcame these struggles, themes reflected in “Plain Jane”.

Drawing from other music academics, Guyton first outlines how hip-hop as a genre began in the 1970s from outdoor parties that took place in a destroyed Bronx neighborhood as a way to promote community and peace, and then how rap became a natural supplement to hip-hop’s flowy beats (Guyton 2021, 5). She then goes on to describe how the genre was slowly adopted by the mainstream, and how the 1980s crack epidemic in America would shift the genre towards rapping about personal struggles and journeys to promote change in black communities Guyton 2021, 10). 

I found Guyton’s argument on how themes in rap emerged strong, as she first presents the evolution of rap and trap music in a narrative manner, highlighting the key historical elements and events that act as underlying causes to the evolution of the genres. She also combines this with direct lyrical examples from rap songs of the time to supplement her argument, which I believe provides even more strength to her argument. This helps to build a sort of inductive argument, where one event builds on the other, to show the growth and evolution of rap from its origin to today. For example, Guyton discusses how, because of the crack epidemic, the LA police would crack down heavily on minority communities, which inspired the massively popular N.W.A. song “Fuck tha Police,” followed by “Fuck the police … They have the authority to kill a minority”, which directly relates to her argument (Guyton 2021, 11).

Additionally, the author strengthened her argument by addressing some counter arguments to her claim. Although such genres of music perpetuated some problematic values, their songs could help support struggling communities and inspire individuals in such communities to try and break out of the place they seem to be trapped in (Guyton 2021, 13). However, there is very little discussion about the positive impacts of rap, such as its role in creating opportunities for struggling individuals in low income communities, as well as its role in promoting community–not only historically but also now. As Guyton states: “Trap music chooses to glamorize the trapping lifestyle, which sometimes leaves little room for the installment of positive messages to its listeners” (Guyton 2021, 28). 

Throughout the reading, Guyton also presents the idea of the “trapped mentality” that many artists and individuals in the genre have. She elaborates on this by saying “Many trappers escaped the trap by pursuing rap careers, and continue to demonstrate their self-reliant mentality to progress in their new profession. Unfortunately, due to the continuous glamorization of their trap lifestyle and urge to attain this standard of manhood, these artists will remain mentally trapped in this reality” (Guyton 2021, 30). She also argues how, to say relevant, artists "continue to lyrically reflect and maintain their trapping personas in efforts to identify with Trap artists and remain relevant in Trap culture" (Guyton 2021, 29). This is reflected across the genre with songs with common lyrics talking about drugs and glow-ups across many songs, including A$AP Ferg’s “Plain Jane”. While this indeed highlights real-world struggles that affect African-American communities, it perpetuates a hyper-masculine and problematic message to the audiences of this music.

Mikalah Guyton’s analysis of rap culture finds that music provides a way out of difficult circumstances for many, the analysis ultimately reveals a cycle that traps artists with a “trapped mentality” with themes of struggle and problematic themes that perpetuate the genre such as masculinity, drugs, and success. The argument presented helped me gain a deeper understanding of the culture and history behind the genre, as well as some of the artists that A$AP Ferg could have taken inspiration from as he grew up. While there is room for improvement, Guyton uses a strong framework to present information and make logical inferences, ultimately providing an insightful piece into rap history and motifs, things generally hidden behind strong lyrics of struggle and success.
