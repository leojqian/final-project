Breaking Boundaries and Honoring Roots - "Plain Jane" Music Review

In 2017, Harlem-based Darold Ferguson Jr., known by stage name A$AP Ferg, released the mixtape “Still Striving” with the lead single “Plain Jane” under RCA Records. The song adapts samples from Juicy J and Three 6 Mafia’s song “Slob On My Knob” (which would be adapted by Ferg to “Ride with the mob”), and quickly rose to legendary status within the Harlem community, sparking street gatherings and Tiktok trends. This momentum carried the song to be recognized and remixed by other trending artists in the genre, such as Nicki Minaj. The collaboration on “Plain Jane” highlights the impact of male-female collaborations in rap, allowing for Ferg’s unique artistic vision and Minaj’s powerful lyrics to challenge misogynistic trends while maintaining the essence and energy of the original song and the genre as a whole. In doing so, the song helps to popularize a genre that many once considered to be too mature.

Produced by Kirk Knight (also known as Kirk Kneezy), “Plain Jane” features a simple yet infectious bass-heavy beat that gives Ferg’s commanding voice and lyrics space to take center stage. The chant-like structure of the lyrics energizes the track and makes it instantly memorable, engaging listeners from the get-go. Throughout the song, Ferg’s flow is undoubtedly repetitive, which allows the audience to easily catch the lyrics and works to highlight the variations between verses.

The song’s music video works to reinforce the authenticity of Ferg and his music. The video is chaotic, but real. It’s shot in the streets of Harlem, where Ferg grew up and came up, and depicts him riding through the roads alongside a group of people on bikes, which establishes the connection he maintains with his roots, despite the success he has found. Ferg "riding with the mob" alludes to the importance of community for up-and-coming rappers, as through live street performances (something that Ferg still does to give back to his community), these artists are able to show their talent and gain fame (Mutinda 2020). The very real delivery of the music and the very real people it describes leaves a strong impression, reminiscing of the crazy “Harlem Picnics” that Ferg describes as part of his childhood, and draws homage to other artists from the 1980s. This cements Ferg's work in hip-hop tradition, retaining the essense that many hip-hop fans seek.

Another notable characteristic of “Plain Jane” (and Ferg himself) is its sampling of notable artists. As discussed in an article by Abbey Road Institute, sampling in hip-hop a means of storytelling and lineage—allowing artists to trace influences and pay homage. Ferg's lyrics "Yamborghini chain, rest in peace to my superiors", he refers to his mentor and founding A$AP Mob member, A$AP Yams (Abbey Road Institute 2021). Additionally, Ferg’s use of Three 6 Mafia’s “Slob On My Knob” in “Plain Jane” exemplifies this tradition: it bridges Memphis and Harlem rap while grounding Ferg’s modern sound in the legacy of Southern hip-hop. "Plain Jane" takes inspiration from Memphis rap group Three 6 Mafia’s “Slob On My Knob,” where Ferg reworks much of the flow and beat, modernizing it to appeal to the current audience. The adaptation is much more bass-heavy, and ditches many of the smoother, retro sounds that Three 6 Mafia included in the original song. Ferg preserves some of the catchy lyrics from the reference song, such as “Check in with me, and do your job” and “suck a n*gga dick or somethin’,” which he describes as Juicy J’s essence and raw emotion (Ferguson 2017). By preserving some lyrics, Ferg is able to bring some of the southern rap culture to his New York listeners, bridging two different sound identities between two prominent rap epicenters: Memphis and New York (French 2017). This also does a good job of reflecting Ferg’s strong artistic background and vision—he is an innovator, revitalizing older hit tracks and taking inspiration from other great artists (Ismael 2017). Such innovation gives the audience a new perspective on retro sounds that is appealing to modern audiences as well as older hip-hop audience.

Alongside "Plain Jane"'s references to Three 6 Mafia, the collaboration with Nicki Minaj symbolizes a forward movement in terms of lyrics with the rap community. As explored by Terri Adams and Douglass Fuller in their 2006 paper, rap and its subgenres, such as trap, frequently engage users with lyrics that objectify and degrade women (Adams and Fuller 2006). A similar sentiment is also expressed by Regina Bradley in her paper, as she describes the all-too-common hyper-masculization of men and hyper-sexualization of women in the rap genre (Bradley 2015). As "Plain Jane" opens, Ferg drops some bars that many would consider “problematic,” such as “I fuck yo bitch for the irony” (Ferguson 2017), which may be interpreted as objectifying, or belittling women. However, when Minaj comes in, her lyrics work to empower women and turn the tables. She praises herself and other women with descriptions such as “body shaped like Jennie, booty dreamy, waist is teeny” and going on to assert their positions as powerful women: “If I call him then he is coming home running,” (Minaj in Ferguson 2017). This comes in conjunction with the popularity of the #MeToo movement on social media platforms, which encouraged survivors of sexual assault, the majority of whom were women, to open up about their stories. Ferg's song and Minaj’s verse work together to challenge traditional gender roles in rap and also aligns with a broader cultural push toward empowering women, and allows for the inclusion of female listeners in a male-dominated hip-hop genre.

Around this time, “Plain Jane” would also be used as the background music of many body-empowerment short-form videos on the internet; people would use the song to transition from unconfident caricatures of themselves to show off natural beauty features, which gained millions of views on platforms such as TikTok and Instagram. The viral use of “Plain Jane” in such videos shows how the song became a tool for self-expression and empowerment. Undoubtedly, this song was part of, and fueled, a larger widespread cultural change via social media, highlighting the positive impact and forward-thinking ideology A$AP Ferg and Nicki Minaj worked to create through their collaboration on this song.

A$AP Ferg’s song “Plain Jane” highlights his ability to innovate in a genre that some would describe as stagnant and misogynistic. The song is able to entrance and unite its listeners with its hard beat, and draw in a wider audience that would go on the rap genre more accessible. From remastering older hits to pushing a more forward-thinking message to his audience, Ferg works with Minaj to seamlessly innovate by blending the past and present—promoting new messages while maintaining strong ties to his heritage.
